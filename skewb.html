The Skewb is a cube that turns around its corners instead of its faces. Every turn moves half of the puzzle. Learn the moves, then learn to solve it with just 3 algorithms, with a coach to help. Time your Skewb solves in the ⏱ Timer (choose Skewb as the event).

How to read it

Each letter turns the half of the Skewb around one corner. Hold it with white on top and green at the front. Three of the corners are on the bottom:

Rbottom-right-back corner
Lbottom-left-front corner
Bbottom-left-back corner
Utop-left-back corner (the only top one)

A letter alone = turn clockwise, as if you were looking straight at that corner.
' (say "prime") = counter-clockwise. R' undoes R.
There's no R2: three turns bring a corner back where it started, so R R is the same as R'.

y and y' turn the whole Skewb in your hands (white stays on top). y turns it like U on a 3x3, so the front goes to the left.

This is the official WCA notation, the same as the Skewb scrambles in the Timer.

The plan

This beginner method keeps the white layer on top the whole time, so the bottom is yellow. (On your own Skewb you can start with any colour. The steps are the same.)

  1. White layer: the white centre plus the 4 corners around it. No algorithms, just think!
  2. Twist the bottom corners with the Sledgehammer.
  3. Fix the centres: the yellow centre with the Bottom Swap, then the sides with the Side Cycle.

We checked this on a computer against all 3,149,280 ways a Skewb can be mixed up: after the white layer, it always finishes in 6 algorithms or fewer.

1The white layer

Get the white centre on top with 4 white corners around it, so the top is all white. The side colours of those corners must match each other too (two greens next to each other, and so on). The side centres don't matter yet.

  • Start with the white centre and one corner that goes with it, then add corners one at a time.
  • A corner on the bottom can usually go up into its place with just one or two turns. Try R, R', L, L', B and B' and watch where it goes.
  • If you put a corner in and it knocks another one out, undo it and try from a different side.
  • It's never more than 7 moves. If you get stuck, the coach can show you a solution.

Got it? Keep the white layer on top from now on.

2Twist the bottom corners

Good news: the 4 bottom corners are already in the right places! Some may be twisted: their yellow sticker is on the side instead of the bottom. Look at the bottom and count the twisted corners:

  • None: skip to step 3.
  • All 4 twisted: one side has two yellow stickers on its bottom corners. Turn the Skewb (y) so that side is on the right, then do the Sledgehammer. Done!
  • 2 twisted: turn the Skewb so a twisted corner is at the bottom front-right with its yellow sticker facing you. Do the Sledgehammer. Now all 4 are twisted: do the line above.
🔨 Sledgehammer

3Fix the centres

3a. The yellow centre

If the yellow centre isn't on the bottom, turn the Skewb so it's at the front, then do the Bottom Swap. It moves the front centre to the bottom. It's the Sledgehammer, then 4 more moves.

⬇️ Bottom Swap

3b. The side centres

Each side centre should match the corners around it. Count how many side centres are right:

  • All 4: solved! 🎉
  • Just 1: turn the Skewb so the right one is at the front. Do the Side Cycle. Not solved yet? Do it once more.
  • None: do the Side Cycle once from any side. Now exactly 1 will be right, so do the line above.

You'll never see exactly 2 or 3 right. A Skewb can't do that!

🔄 Side Cycle

The front centre stays put. The other three swap round: back → right → left → back.
